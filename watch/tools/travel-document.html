Travel document check

Reads the machine-readable zone (MRZ) of a passport, ID card or visa and checks it against the ICAO Doc 9303 rules: check digits, dates, codes and layout.
Works only in your browser. Nothing you type is saved, sent or logged; the page has no server behind it. A passing check only means the MRZ is internally consistent: forgers can compute check digits too. Always compare with the printed page, the chip and official systems.

1. Type or paste the MRZ

Specimens from ICAO Doc 9303 (fictional "Utopia"):
Passports have 2 lines of 44 characters; ID cards 3 lines of 30; some cards and visas 2 lines of 36. Use < for the filler character. Letter O and digit 0 are often confused: the check digits will catch it.

What to check next (whatever the result)

  1. Visual zone vs MRZ: name, number, dates and nationality on the printed page must match the MRZ exactly.
  2. Chip: if the passport has a chip (the camera-like symbol on the cover), read it; chip data must match the page and the photo.
  3. Lost and stolen documents: check the number through your agency's official systems (for example INTERPOL SLTD through the national bureau).
  4. Security features: compare with genuine specimens in a public reference such as PRADO (EU Council): laminate, printing, page numbering, perforations, ultraviolet features.
  5. The holder: compare the photo and features; ask about the stated travel history and check stamps and visas against it.
  6. Record what you checked and the result, following your agency's procedure.

Unofficial tool, part of Border Watch OSINT (open-source only, not a government service). Rules: ICAO Doc 9303, Machine Readable Travel Documents, 8th edition.
