How to read this site

About 5 minutes. Read this once before using the dashboard.

1What this site is

Border Watch collects open-source news and official public releases about travel documents, migration, trafficking, smuggling, seizures and border security, worldwide with a Caribbean focus. It updates every morning.

It is an unofficial project. It is not a government service, uses no case files or official data, and is not linked to any agency's systems. Every item is a lead to check, not a finding.

2Review priority = what to read first

Review priority tells you what to read first. It never means a person, country or region is risky.
HighMediumLowUpdate
  • Only incidents (a crime or enforcement action, such as an arrest, seizure or fake document) are scored, from words in the report such as the amount seized, weapons, or an INTERPOL notice. Being about Jamaica or the Caribbean adds no points; those stories are shown in their own lists instead.
  • Update = news with no specific crime: a new visa rule, a travel advisory, an agreement. Not scored.
  • Nationality is never part of the score.
  • Reviewed appears only on reports a person has checked. Everything else was sorted automatically.

3How the charts count

  • The charts count stories, not people, seizures or cases. A story about three Haitians adds 1 to Haiti.
  • Activity over time, Review priority and Main category count each story once, under its main category, so they add up to the same total (the blue "N stories" badge).
  • Countries named and Nationality count a story once for each country it names, so they do not add up to the total.
  • Click a bar, or the name beside it, to see the stories behind it. "Filter the whole page" applies it everywhere.
  • The filters at the top (section, Caribbean / World / Jamaica, period) apply to every chart.

4Cautions

  • News is not ground truth. It reflects what journalists and agencies chose to publish. A rise in reports can mean more media attention, not more activity.
  • Sorting is automatic and can be wrong. It works by matching words, not by understanding. Expect some off-topic stories and some misses.
  • Check the original article and official sources before acting on anything here. Seizure totals and figures are as reported, not verified.
  • Coverage is uneven. Some languages and countries are covered better than others (see Language coverage in the side menu).

5Where to start

  1. Overview: the latest developments and the high-priority reports for the Caribbean.
  2. Open the section that interests you: Passports & Documents, Immigration, Trafficking, Seizures & Contraband and others.
  3. Use search for a place, airport, airline, document type or flight number.
  4. Open a story's Full record to see what was extracted and the link to the original article.
  5. Use the Tools for reference: Travel document check, Visa requirements, Flight tracker, Port and Route lookup.
